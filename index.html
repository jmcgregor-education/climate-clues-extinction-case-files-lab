Reading the Record: Climate Clues & Extinction Case Files

Your assignment

Geologists can’t travel back in time, but they can read what the rocks recorded. In this lab you’ll work two stations, the same way a geologist would:

  1. Station 1: Climate detective. Read a stack of rock layers, a seafloor core, and ice core and modern CO₂ data to reconstruct how the climate has changed.
  2. Station 2: Extinction case files. Sort the evidence from a mass extinction boundary, then identify a second extinction from its evidence.

Your score updates as you go. When you finish, open the Lab report tab, save it as a PDF, and turn it in.

Station 2 unlocks when you finish Station 1. You can retry anything, but first-try answers earn the most points. The sites in this lab are made up, but the clues are the real kinds of evidence geologists use.

Case file B: A different boundary

Field report from a second boundary:

  • Fusulinids, trilobites, and many kinds of coral are common below the boundary. None are found above it.
  • Huge stacks of flood basalt in Siberia are dated to the same time as the boundary.
  • Oxygen isotopes in shells show the oceans warmed by several degrees right at the boundary.
  • Black shale with pyrite covers the boundary at many ocean sites.
  • No iridium spike, shocked quartz, or glass spherules have been found in the boundary layer.
